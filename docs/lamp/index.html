Each picture is 145 seconds of it at the “slow!” setting, read left to right and wrapping at the end of every row, 4.7 ms per pixel, with a black row between passes. Colour comes from the datasheet's forward voltages and luminous intensities through the CIE 1931 observer; brightness is compressed the way a dark-adapted eye compresses it.

“Switches” means something is dark more than a quarter of the time — the trajectory turning a colour off as it changes wings. “Always lit” means all three dies stay above their turn-on voltage and the colour wanders instead of blinking.

Every option is buildable as drawn: the reference divider hangs off the ±12 V rails and is buffered by the half of U2 that Paul never needed, and the common-anode ones are the same package with the pin 1 and pin 4 connections exchanged.
